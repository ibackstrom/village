Village Glade

Research notes and an implementation plan for a warmer, more interesting, and more reliable edition of the village generator.

Creative boundary: this is an original interpretation of broad cozy-diorama and procedural-layout principles. It does not copy Tiny Glade assets, code, interface, trademarks, or proprietary building systems. Tiny Glade is used only as a public design reference.

What the strongest references agree on

Relationships beat decoration

The memorable effect is not simply pastel color. Elements react to context: a path meets a door, a raised volume gets supports, and nearby parts visually belong together. In this generator, roads define blocks first; fixed-size buildings are then oriented and fitted inside those blocks.

Every rule is curated

Pounce Light describes procedural art as many hand-authored rules, with a different pipeline for each element—not one magic algorithm. This supports retaining specialized generators for roads, walls, buildings, water, and vegetation.

A scene should suggest stories

Landmarks, bridges, courtyards, clustered homes, rivers, trees, and changing moods create useful narrative prompts. Quantity alone does not. A compact village with readable neighborhoods is stronger than undifferentiated density.

Flow is a feature

Fast response and forgiving controls protect the cozy feeling. Procedural glitches, overlaps, and surprising state changes interrupt it. Deterministic seeds, fixed proxy footprints, an unchanged fallback version, and browser-native downloads therefore matter as much as style.

What this edition implements

  • A small composed village: walls, castle, church, market, internal roads, river, blocks, and greenery are enabled by default.
  • Fixed production geometry: ordinary buildings still use the eight exact footprints documented for Houdini and Unreal. The visual treatment does not alter exported scale.
  • Readable blocks: faint block boundaries preserve the relationship between street network and building clusters.
  • Cozy elevation cues: raised walls, outlined gable roofs, warm roof variation, district tinting, tree clusters, soft contrast, paper grain, and edge vignette add a diorama-like hierarchy without pretending to be 3D.
  • Three authored moods: Meadow, Harvest, and Twilight change a controlled palette rather than randomizing every color.
  • Calmer controls: the small overlay offers only mood, new seed, quiet view, original version, and ZIP. Detailed generation controls remain in the map's existing Menu/Warp workflow.
  • Stable interchange: CSV export, names, rotations, wall roles, roads, river points, and Houdini/Unreal compatibility are inherited from the stable build.
  • Isolation and fallback: this edition has its own saved-settings namespace and lives under glade/; it cannot overwrite the stable generator's local preferences.

Reliability decisions

  • The existing road-and-block topology remains the source of truth. Style changes do not introduce a second geometry system that could drift away from CSV.
  • Buildings are accepted only when a complete standard footprint fits inside its generated lot. This guards against the most visible boundary intersections.
  • The building amount is bounded, and village topology scales with it instead of filling arbitrary points.
  • Landmarks remain specialized geometry and are exported under explicit names.
  • Downloads use a real temporary anchor and object URL, including a UTF-8 BOM for CSV compatibility in Windows tools.
  • The original generator is preserved unchanged as the production fallback.

Highest-value next steps

  1. Adjacency grammar: classify each building edge as street, neighbor, courtyard, wall, or water. Use that classification to reserve doors on streets, shared walls between side-by-side houses, and gardens in rear setbacks.
  2. Block occupancy solver: pack standard modules along street fronts using intervals, then validate every footprint and clearance with a spatial index. This would make terraces intentional instead of accidental.
  3. Road conflict pass: classify crossings, T-junctions, gates, and bridges; remove tiny road stubs and merge near-collinear segments before export.
  4. Wall chemistry: derive straight, turn, gate, tower-end, and bridge interfaces from neighboring segments, with modular lengths matched to the Houdini kit.
  5. Story details as data: export optional points for wells, carts, gardens, trees, benches, lamps, and washing lines, each constrained by clearance and neighborhood type.
  6. Reproducible validation: batch-generate hundreds of seeds and reject footprints crossing roads, water, walls, or other buildings. Keep screenshots for the worst layouts rather than testing only attractive seeds.

Sources and how they informed the work

  • Tiny Glade official Steam page — gridless “building chemistry,” procedural detail, no failure states, and an alive diorama atmosphere.
  • Pounce Light interview, 80 Level — hand-curated procedural rules, specialized generators, iterative UX testing, story-enabling tools, and the importance of preventing glitches from breaking creative flow.
  • Digital Foundry technical analysis — warm cohesive lighting, low-complexity proxy geometry, fast feedback, procedural micro-detail, water highlights, and diorama depth cues.
  • Parish & Müller, Procedural Modeling of Cities — road networks and population/environment constraints as an organizing layer.
  • Citygen — hierarchical road generation, lots, and building placement as separate but connected stages.
  • City generation notes by Martin Devans — practical interpretation of road, block, and lot subdivision.
  • Watabou / Procgen Arcana — the original city topology, map renderer, and interaction foundation used by this experimental derivative.

The architectural references supplied with the project were also reviewed as inspiration for an eventual offline Houdini detail pass. They are not treated as implementation specifications for this browser build.
